# Historical Preface: The Scene — January 30, 1939

Berlin, Monday, the 30th of January 1939. Six years to the day since it all began — and the city knows it before it wakes up. Here is what you are actually looking at, and the parts almost nobody knows.

**First, the date itself.** January 30 was to this government what July 4 is to America — its birthday as a holiday, celebrated every year from 1933 to the very end. Every January 30, across every town and city in Germany, there were torch-lit processions deliberately restaging the night of 1933, when tens of thousands of SA and SS men had marched through the Brandenburg Gate carrying flame, hours after Hitler was sworn in as Chancellor. The ritual was the point: fire, columns of marchers, the radio carrying the ceremonies into every home, party members and civil servants assembled in halls to listen together. So the day of this speech is not just a date on a calendar — it is the founding festival, deliberately chosen so that everything said that evening would carry the weight of destiny. Which is exactly why Hitler opens the speech the way he does, taking the room back to that torch-lit evening in 1933 before saying a single word about the present.

**Now, the place — and this is the detail almost everyone gets wrong.** Picture the scene in your mind and you will probably imagine the great glass dome of the Reichstag. But that building had been a burnt-out shell for almost six years. On the night of February 27, 1933 — less than a month after Hitler took power — the Reichstag caught fire (a Dutch communist, Marinus van der Lubbe, was arrested inside; who exactly set it remains debated to this day), and the fire gutted the debating chamber. The parliament never met there again. Instead, starting just days later, the German Reichstag convened inside the **Kroll Opera House** — the Krolloper — a grand old opera house on Königsplatz, directly across the square from the blackened ruin of the real parliament.

And here is the part that feels like a novelist's invention: the Krolloper had its own wild history. Built in the 1840s as a commercial rival to the royal opera, it went through the usual ups and downs, and then, in 1927, it became the most scandalous theater in Europe — the "Kroll Oper," where the director Otto Klemperer (yes, the famous conductor, and no relation to Victor Klemperer, the diarist we will meet later) staged avant-garde, modernist opera that conservative Berlin howled about. Mozart with jazz interludes, unorthodox staging, debates in the press for years. That stage — the stage where Berlin's cultural battles of the late Weimar years had been fought — was the very stage where, on March 23, 1933, the Reichstag passed the Enabling Act, the law that voted German democracy out of existence, handing Hitler's cabinet the power to legislate without parliament. And it was the stage where, on this January evening in 1939, with that same ruin of a parliament building dark across the plaza, the "first Reichstag of Greater Germany" assembled to hear him speak. The symbolism was not subtle, and nobody present missed it.

**Now the people — and how they got there, which is stranger than you would think.** The body sitting in that hall numbered **855 deputies**. Here is what that means in practice, because most people do not know how this parliament worked by 1939. There had been no contested election in Germany for years. In the election of April 10, 1938 — held just three weeks after the Anschluss, deliberately fused with the Austria plebiscite — voters were presented with a single list of 814 National Socialist candidates, and the ballot was not even secret in practice: you marked your paper openly, and an invalid or "no" ballot was visible to anyone watching. Nearly 99% approved. That was the whole Reichstag: 814 men. Then, on December 4, 1938, after the Sudetenland was taken, 41 deputies from the Sudeten German Nazi party — Konrad Henlein's people, brought into the fold — were simply added to the roll. No vote. Eight hundred and fifty-five. By the standards of this state, they were a remarkably diverse crowd: Nazi functionaries, yes, but also holdover nationalists, a few token non-party "guests" of the old conservative and even former Centre stripe, sitting in a chamber that had not meaningfully legislated since March 1933. This was the parliament's **twelfth session in six years**. Think about that. Twelve sittings in six years. Its real function was exactly what it did that night: assemble, listen, acclaim, disperse.

Presiding over it all from the chair was **Hermann Göring**, Reichstag President since August 1932 — which is a delicious historical footnote, because he had been elected to that post by the *last freely elected* Reichstag of the Weimar Republic, and simply kept the job through the entire transformation. In the gallery: the diplomatic corps, foreign correspondents, cameras. Out beyond the hall, the actual audience — the whole country.

**Because the real venue was the radio.** This is the thing to understand about 1939: almost nobody you knew personally was in the Krolloper, but virtually everybody in Germany could hear it, live, that evening. The Reich Broadcasting Corporation carried the speech across the whole country, and it went out on shortwave to the world. In homes, people gathered around their Volksempfänger — the cheap "people's receiver" the government had pushed into millions of living rooms. In factories and offices, loudspeakers. In town squares, speaker columns. Party and state workplaces had been told to assemble their people to listen. So when you imagine the audience, do not picture 855 men in a hall; picture a nation sitting in the glow of radio dials on a cold Monday night — which was precisely the arrangement the Propaganda Ministry, under Joseph Goebbels, had spent years perfecting. Goebbels had helped write the speech, and his ministry choreographed everything around it: the press embargo, the live broadcast, the newsreel cameras.

**And the cameras!** This is another under-known fact. The speech was recorded — sound and picture — for the weekly newsreel that every German would see before the feature film at the cinema the following week. The newsreel companies had been struggling for years to record usable sound of a full indoor speech, and this time they got it: the prophecy passage survives on film, in UFA's newsreel number 439 for 1939, one of the earliest substantial film-and-sound records of Hitler addressing the Reichstag. Most Germans never saw him in person; what they saw was this — the close-ups, the raised hand, the famous passages in his voice, in the dark of their local cinema.

**The weather, honestly told.** The exact thermometer reading for January 30, 1939 did not surface in the surviving sources consulted, and it will not be invented here. What the records do show: mid-January 1939 was freakishly mild for Berlin — the Dahlem weather station logged about 13 degrees Celsius on January 18, a decadal record. The notorious winter — the kind that freezes history — came a year later, in early 1940. So picture the capital in that in-between late-January state: around freezing, damp, dark by late afternoon, the kind of evening where an overheated opera house feels like shelter and a radio fireside feels like company.

**What was actually said — and why each part was there.** The speech runs two to two and a half hours, and it is constructed like a cathedral.

He begins with memory: that torch-lit night six years before, the 13 million voters who stood behind him then, the Jews and the Marxists who laughed at the idea of rescue, and the "turn of fortune through God's providence." He is not speaking to the hall; he is speaking to the radio listener in the armchair, confirming the founding myth.

Then the year 1938, told as destiny: the decision taken "in January 1938" to solve Austria "one way or another"; Berchtesgaden with Schuschnigg; the night of March 9, the mobilization order, the entry into Austria — he recites the timetable like a commander after a campaign — and the Sudeten crisis, Munich, Mussolini, Chamberlain, Daladier. He thanks Göring for the Luftwaffe's air armament drive and **Joachim von Ribbentrop**, his Foreign Minister, by name. And he crowns the year as "the triumph of an idea" — a people united not by the sword but by conviction, the flag of the Reich carried "in their hearts" by the Austrians and Sudeten Germans.

Then comes the long, strange middle — and this is the part almost nobody reads today, though contemporaries had to sit through it. A lecture on economics. He tells the Reichstag, and through it the world, that Germany is "the strongest people of Central Europe" forced by the peace settlement to export or starve — *"German people live, that is, export, or die."* He works through the Versailles reparations (the "truly insane reparation sums" of 1919-20, the loans that propped German competition up only to destroy it), the stolen colonies (he does actual accounting: keeping Germany's three million square kilometers of colonies, he estimates, will cost England "more than 20 billion gold marks"), the gold-standard theories of the "financial and economic theologians" who predicted Germany's collapse every quarter. He lays out the Four Year Plan, the Reichsbank reform, wage theory. Why? Because in 1939 the world's great question was whether the German experiment would collapse or be strangled — and this speech's buried headline, which serious observers absolutely caught, was the open declaration that Germany's food problem could only be solved "on two paths" — import, or **"the expansion of the living space of our people."** That is Lebensraum, stated to parliament in the middle of a budget argument. Six weeks later, German troops occupied Prague.

Then the foreign-policy passages, naming names. **Duff Cooper** — the British First Lord of the Admiralty who had resigned in protest over Munich, an authentic hero of conscience in London, named here as a "war apostle." **Anthony Eden**, the elegant Foreign Secretary who had resigned over policy toward Italy. **Winston Churchill**, then a mistrusted backbench voice warning of German rearmament, named alongside them. And "M. Ickes" — almost nobody today knows that one: **Harold Ickes**, President Roosevelt's Secretary of the Interior, who had attacked Nazism publicly in a 1938 speech and was, to Berlin, the voice of an America sliding toward intervention. The claim that Germany planned to conquer "North or South America, Australia, China or even the Netherlands" is answered with mockery: the only thing left to add, he says, is the prophecy that Germany intends "immediately to occupy the full moon." The Americans had been fed such stories, he says, by a Jewish-controlled press — and then he lands the counter-history: it was not Germany that attacked America in 1917 but the reverse, "without any compelling reason, solely for capitalist reasons," as proven — this part is real, and audacious to cite — by the investigations of the **American Congress's own Nye Committee**, which had spent the mid-1930s digging into how the United States entered the Great War.

And then the passage the whole speech is now remembered for. He turns to the Jewish question — and here the context matters enormously, because most retellings strip it out. Ten weeks earlier, on the nights of November 9-10, 1938, the Nazi government had carried out Kristallnacht: synagogues burned across Germany, thousands of Jewish shops smashed, some thirty thousand Jewish men dragged to concentration camps, and the community fined a billion marks for the damage done to it. Germany's Jews were now desperate to emigrate — and at that very moment, in those very weeks, a real negotiation was underway in Berlin between **George Rublee**, the American lawyer leading the international refugee committee set up after the Évian Conference, and Hitler's own Economics Ministry, over a scheme (soon called the Rublee Plan) under which Jews could leave with a portion of their assets. It is that negotiation the speech directly addresses and savages: the democracies "assure us" they cannot take the Jews "unless they receive from Germany a certain capital sum for emigration" — and this, he says, Germany will not do; instead Germany "makes good again only what this people itself has incurred." He recites Germany's grievances — the inflation of 1923 "instigated by Jews," the reparations, the "almost a million dairy cows" driven out of a starving Germany under the treaty terms, the prisoners held after the Armistice, the expulsions from the borderlands. And then the demand: Germany's affairs will not be ruled by a foreign people; "France for the French, England for the English, America for the Americans, and Germany for the Germans!" Foreign culture out of German hands. Let the democracies take these "splendid culture-bearers" in, he taunts — they refuse.

And then the prophet. He reminds them he has often prophesied and was laughed at — in the Kampfzeit the Jews laughed loudest at the prophecy that he would rule Germany and "bring the Jewish problem to a solution." That laughter, he says, has stuck in their throats. And then, slowly enough for every microphone and camera:

*"Today I will once more be a prophet: if the international Jewish financiers inside and outside Europe succeed in plunging the nations once more into a world war, then the result will not be the Bolshevization of the earth and thus the victory of Jewry, but the annihilation of the Jewish race in Europe."*

Delivered calmly, in the middle of a two-hour policy speech, to a hall that had just heard about dairy cows and currency theory.

He is not finished. There is the church section — which needs explaining too, because in 1939 the Nazi government and the churches were in open friction: the Pope's 1937 encyclical *Mit brennender Sorge* had condemned the treatment of the Church; trials of priests and nuns were in the papers; foreign Catholic opinion was hostile. So Hitler pauses to swear a "solemn declaration" before the whole German people: nobody in Germany is persecuted for religion; the state has given the churches, he says, hundreds of millions of marks a year (he reads the figures from a prepared table — 130 million in 1933 rising to 500 million in 1938 — the very table preserved in the pamphlet transcribed in this volume); any priest is free to serve God, but a priest who acts as a political enemy "we will annihilate." He mocks the democracies who wept for German priests while silent over priests massacred in Russia and Spain, and he praises the Germans and Italians who went to Franco's Spain. Then the finale of foreign policy: the friendship with Mussolini's Italy — he invokes their "community" born of a thousand years of contact and the common danger of Bolshevism — and the Anti-Comintern Pact with Japan, a real treaty from 1937 against the spread of communism, which he casts as the seed of a world grouping. He reaffirms the non-aggression pact with Poland (January 26, 1934 — that week was its fifth anniversary; in six months he would invade). He brushes off American slander, wishes America peace — and closes by sanctifying the hour: the great Reich as the vessel of a thousand years of German struggle, the dukes and kings and emperors of history as mere "tools of Providence," and the thanks to God "that He has blessed our generation and us, to experience this time and this hour."

Göring rises to close the session, and his words — preserved in the stenographic record, though the printed pamphlet transcribed in this volume stops before them — deserve to be part of the picture: *"My Führer! The fighters of the first hour are assembled before you... Sieg Heil! Sieg Heil! Sieg Heil!"* Three times, in unison, in the old opera house.

**The mood — three different evenings, really.** Inside the Krolloper: ritual warmth, certainty, triumph. The deputies had lived through the miracle year of 1938; everything had worked; the hall adored him. On the radio, in the cold flats and factory canteens of Germany, it was something else — long. The underground Social Democrats' confidential reports from inside Germany found that many listeners took the speech as an omen of *war*, a trumpet before the storm; others simply endured it as they endured all broadcasts, the economic middle of it going over most heads. And the world? Here is the fact that still stuns, and it is one historians keep confirming: **almost nobody treated the prophecy as the news.** The foreign press and the diplomats in the gallery fastened on what seemed urgent — the passages on Italy and Mussolini, the colonial accounting, the tone toward Poland, the America passages. The sentence promising annihilation was reported, in the main, as one item among many. The diaries that survive from ordinary Germans — Victor Klemperer's in Dresden, Luise Solmitz's in Hamburg — mention the day in passing, without a tremor about what it would come to mean. Goebbels logged the day in his diary as a matter of routine. The audience had, once again, laughed at the prophet. He noticed. Two years later, on the same date, he would throw it back at them: the Jews, he said then, "may still be laughing about it today, just as they laughed about my earlier prophecies." By the third January 30 of the war, in 1942, with the machinery of murder already running, he named the prophecy from that opera-house stage as history fulfilling itself — and historians ever since have treated that January evening as one of the hinge moments of the century, a program spoken aloud while the hall applauded and the world checked its watch.

**And the paper trail.** Within days, the text presented in this volume was set in type — the pamphlet by M. Müller & Sohn of Berlin SW 68, with its subsidy table and even its little printer's slip ("xergeblich" where "vergeblich" should be), distributed, quoted on wall newspapers, excerpted on posters, quoted back by its author for six years. The broadcast recording went into the archives. The newsreel went into every cinema. And the sentence itself went into history — on January 30, 1939, in an opera house in Berlin, on a cold, ordinary, unforgettable Monday night.

**Where this account is honest about the gaps:** the exact hour the session began, the precise temperature that day, the attendance roll of which of the 855 were physically present, and Göring's verbatim *opening* words — these could not be documented, and they have not been invented. Everything else above is on the record.

<a id="en-140"></a>[**[140]**](#de-140)  To this was then added the further fact that one believed one already saw in the Germany of that time one of the factors of the aspired Jewish world domination as ultimately not quite compliant, and therefore also endeavored from this side, with all means, to incite to the struggle against Germany.

<a id="en-143"></a>[**[143]**](#de-143)  After the armistice these obligations were broken in the most shameful manner. Here now began the madness of the so-called victor states of transforming the suffering of war into their permanent war of peace.

<a id="en-144"></a>[**[144]**](#de-144)  If this condition today is removed for the greatest part, then not at all out of reasons of insight, or even of mere fairness, on the part of the democratic statesmen, but exclusively through the strength of the German nation, which has come to itself again.

<a id="en-146"></a>[**[146]**](#de-146)  The ingenious British economic journalists, who had previously written that the annihilation of Germany would increase the wealth of every individual Englishman and redound to the welfare of their country, had to keep silent for at least a certain time after the war — chastised as too great liars by reality.

<a id="en-147"></a>[**[147]**](#de-147)  Only in the last months do similarly ingenious insights begin to surface again in the speeches of British politicians and in the leading articles of such-like newspaper writers.

<a id="en-148"></a>[**[148]**](#de-148)  Why was the World War fought? To destroy the German sea power, which stood in second place? <a id="en-150"></a>[**[150]**](#de-150)  From the shores of the Pacific Ocean in the East of Asia to the floods of the North Sea and to the coasts of the Mediterranean, other state systems are spreading with rapid swiftness. <a id="en-151"></a>[**[151]**](#de-151)  It will, for example, for all the future be the proof of an extraordinarily limited insight, precisely in the judgment of economic possibilities, to look at the sums which were estimated in the years 1919 and 1920 as possible reparations performances. <a id="en-152"></a>[**[152]**](#de-152)  For the situation was as follows: first one waged the war in order to exclude Germany from world trade. In the pursuit of this war aim, the conclusion of peace would thereby have had to transform Germany into an autarky. That is to say, the other states, which felt themselves threatened by German world trade, would after the end of the war have had to place at the disposal of the German people a space suited to an autarkic mode of life, with the stipulation that it now seek its livelihood therein and no longer touch the rest of the world economically.

<a id="en-153"></a>[**[153]**](#de-153)  This one did not do; instead, one waged a world war in order to exclude Germany from world trade — that was the sole real reason for the behavior of the warmongers of that time — and now obligated the defeated state with an international reparations burden which could be realized only through a virtually doubled activity on the world market.

<a id="en-154"></a>[**[154]**](#de-154)  Yes, not only that: in order to hold Germany back from every autarkic activity, or to hinder it therein, one goes and takes from the Reich even its own colonial possessions, once acquired through purchase and treaties.

<a id="en-156"></a>[**[156]**](#de-156)  For now the German export must not only suffice to satisfy German needs, but to generate in addition truly insane reparation sums, whereby of course, in order to deliver one mark, practically 3 and 4 marks had to be exported, for in the long run these gigantic sums could be rendered only from profit and not out of any substance.

<a id="en-157"></a>[**[157]**](#de-157)  Since Germany was not in a position to meet these obligations, the victor nations even propped up, through loans, the emergence of German economic competition on the world market, for whose cleansing from the German commercial enemy 10 or 12 million people had previously had to bleed on the battlefields.

<a id="en-158"></a>[**[158]**](#de-158)  That this madness finally led to an escalation and ultimately threw all national economies into confusion, forcing the gravest currency crises, may be mentioned only in passing.

<a id="en-159"></a>[**[159]**](#de-159)  The whole behavior of the so-called victor powers after the end of the World War was just as unreasonable as irresponsible. <a id="en-160"></a>[**[160]**](#de-160)  In the year 1918 one truly had, after the end of the war, the authority to bring about a reasonable settlement of international problems. <a id="en-163"></a>[**[163]**](#de-163)  How can a just and reasonable participation of all great nations in the riches of the world be secured? <a id="en-166"></a>[**[166]**](#de-166)  But to assume that it is permitted to certain peoples by the good Lord first to take possession of a world by force and then to defend the robbery with moral theories is perhaps reassuring for the possessor and above all convenient, but for the non-possessor just as irrelevant as uninteresting and non-binding!

<a id="en-167"></a>[**[167]**](#de-167)  Nor is the problem solved by a very great statesman then simply declaring, with a scornful grin, that there are nations which are the haves, and that the others therefore are now once and for all the have-nots.

<a id="en-170"></a>[**[170]**](#de-170)  It is conceivable that in the course of long periods of time peoples, through internal crises, seem to step aside temporarily from the stage of historical events; but to believe that in Europe peoples like the Germans or Italians would for eternal times disappear as equally entitled appearances of history — and indeed not only as passive, but also as active forces of the shaping of life — would probably be a very great fallacy.

<a id="en-171"></a>[**[171]**](#de-171)  The situation, so far as it concerns Germany, is a very simple one. The Reich numbers 80 million people today. That is over 135 per square kilometer. The great German colonial possession, which the Reich once acquired in peacetime through treaties and purchase, has been stolen, and indeed contrary to the solemn assurances of the American President Wilson, which formed the basis of our laying down of arms.

<a id="en-172"></a>[**[172]**](#de-172)  The objection that this colonial possession would in any case be of no significance could only lead to returning it to us all the more with a light heart. The objection that this is not possible because Germany would not know what to do with it, since it had earlier done nothing with it either, is ridiculous.

<a id="en-173"></a>[**[173]**](#de-173)  Germany received its colonial possession only late, was able to develop it in a relatively short time, and did not stand before the war in the compelling need of today. This objection is therefore exactly as stupid as if one disputed a people's capacity to build a railway because a hundred years ago it had as yet no railway.

<a id="en-174"></a>[**[174]**](#de-174)  The further objection that the colonial possession could not be returned because Germany would thereby obtain a strategic position is an outrageous attempt to contest, from the very outset, the general rights of a nation, of a people.

<a id="en-175"></a>[**[175]**](#de-175)  For it can only be a matter of that. Germany was in any case the only state which had not raised a colonial army, in reliance on the provisions of the Congo Act, later broken by the Allies. But Germany does not need its colonial possession at all in order to set up armies there; for that the popular wealth of our own race suffices; rather, for its economic relief.

<a id="en-176"></a>[**[176]**](#de-176)  But if one should not believe us in this, then this is wholly irrelevant and changes nothing in our right. <a id="en-177"></a>[**[177]**](#de-177)  It is simply the case that in the long run a nation of 80 million does not want to be valued otherwise than any other people. All these arguments show, in their untruth and wretchedness, all the more clearly that at bottom it is only a question of power, in which reason and justice fall wholly by the wayside.

<a id="en-178"></a>[**[178]**](#de-178)  For from the standpoint of reason, the same reasons which could once be advanced against the robbery of the colonies speak today for the return of the same. <a id="en-179"></a>[**[179]**](#de-179)  For about one thing precisely those countries must surely be clear which themselves dispose over the enormous economic possibilities, whether in consequence of their own extension of the motherland or in consequence of large additional colonial territories: that without sufficient food supply and without certain unavoidably necessary raw materials the economic existence of a people cannot be maintained.

<a id="en-180"></a>[**[180]**](#de-180)  If both are lacking, one thereby compels a people, under all circumstances, to participate in the world economy, and indeed in a measure which may then perhaps even be inconvenient to other states.

<a id="en-181"></a>[**[181]**](#de-181)  Only a few years ago, when under the compulsion of circumstances Germany undertook the Four Year Plan, we were able, to our great astonishment, to hear from the mouths of English politicians and statesmen the then so sincerely sounding, regretful reproach that Germany was thereby detaching itself from the magic circle of the international world economy, indeed of world-economic relations altogether, and was thus entering into a regrettable isolation. I put it to Mr. Eden that this fear was probably somewhat exaggerated and, if sincerely meant at all, not accurate.

<a id="en-182"></a>[**[182]**](#de-182)  Today's circumstances make it quite impossible for Germany to withdraw from the world economy. <a id="en-183"></a>[**[183]**](#de-183)  The reproach that through the German methods of a mutual exchange of goods world trade is moving under the sign of a step backward could — if correct at all — then strike only those who bear the guilt for this development. <a id="en-184"></a>[**[184]**](#de-184)  Under these circumstances, however, the German system — to give for honestly performed work an equally honestly earned counter-performance — is a more decent practice than the payment in foreign exchange, which a year later is devalued by such-and-such a percent.

<a id="en-185"></a>[**[185]**](#de-185)  If certain countries combat the German method, it happens probably in the first instance for the reason that through this German manner of regulating commercial traffic the practices of international currency and stock-exchange speculation have been eliminated in favor of an honest trading business.

<a id="en-198"></a>[**[198]**](#de-198)  In other countries one goes the opposite ways. One diminishes production, raises the national income through rising wages, thereby lowers the purchasing power of money, and finally lands at the devaluation of the currency.

<a id="en-200"></a>[**[200]**](#de-200)  Only then, when the last labor power in Germany is accommodated, will every further increase of the total labor performance, which then arises additionally, be it through an intensive labor or through a higher genius-ization of the technique of labor, lead to an increased participation of the individual in the now more highly made-possible consumption, and thereby to a practical wage increase.

<a id="en-205"></a>[**[205]**](#de-205)  2. the expansion of the living space of our people, in order thereby to secure, in the internal circulation of our economy, the problem of the nutrition of Germany.

<a id="en-207"></a>[**[207]**](#de-207)  This compulsion is accordingly not a capitalist one, as may perhaps be the case in other countries, but the hardest need that can befall a people, namely the care for the daily bread.

<a id="en-208"></a>[**[208]**](#de-208)  And if here foreign statesmen now threaten with I-know-not-what economic countermeasures, then I can only assure that in such a case an economic struggle of despair would set in, which for us is very easily fought through. <a id="en-229"></a>[**[229]**](#de-229)  For the time of German defenselessness was not identical with the highest international, political, or indeed only economic equality; on the contrary: it was the time of the most humiliating treatment ever meted out to a great people, as well as of the worst extortion.

<a id="en-232"></a>[**[232]**](#de-232)  In certain democracies it apparently belongs to the special prerogatives of the politically democratic life to breed artificially the hatred against the so-called totalitarian states, that is, through a flood of reports partly distorting, partly freely invented altogether, to excite public opinion against peoples which had done nothing to harm the other peoples and also wish to do nothing to harm them, which at most themselves were for decades oppressed by heavy injustice.

# Appendix: The Origin of the Swastika

The oldest known swastika in the world was carved onto a bird-shaped figurine made of mammoth ivory, found at **Mezin (Mizyn), in Ukraine**, on the Desna River — a campsite of mammoth hunters from the late Ice Age, with circular dwellings framed in mammoth bones. The figurine is roughly **15,000 years old**. The bird wears an intricate band of joined meanders — and in that band, archaeologists recognize the earliest depiction of a swastika anywhere on Earth. Bracelets of mammoth bone from the same site carry the same hooked-arms motif. Nobody knows what it meant to those hunters. What we do know is that a geometrical idea that easy to invent — a cross with bent arms, a spinning wheel — seems to have occurred to human beings again and again, on every inhabited continent, for the next fourteen and a half millennia.

By the Bronze Age it is everywhere. Seals of the **Indus Valley civilization** (roughly 2500–2000 BCE, Mohenjo-daro and Harappa) carry swastikas. In **ancient Greece** it becomes a standard ornament on pots from the 8th century BCE onward — the Greeks called it the *gammadion*, the "gamma-cross," because its arms look like stacked Greek gammas. **Roman** floor mosaics, **Celtic** metalwork, **Slavic** embroidery, gold bracteates of the Germanic Migration Age in Scandinavia — the hooked cross keeps turning up, and in nearly every setting it belongs to the same family of meanings: the sun, motion, luck, life, protection. It appears in the Roman **catacombs** as an early Christian funerary sign (one inscription reads "Life of Life"), and it was woven into the **Navajo** world as the "whirling logs," into Hopi tradition, into Akan and Tlingit art, and into pre-Columbian **Peruvian textiles**. Before the twentieth century, the swastika was a near-universal sign of blessing.

## The Word, and What It Means

"Swastika" is a **Sanskrit word**, not a German one. It comes from *su-* (good, well) and *asti* (to be) — literally "well-being," "it is good." In the sacred traditions of India it is an emblem of auspiciousness: in **Hinduism** it stands at thresholds, on account books at the New Year, before images of Ganesha, and some say it encodes the four Vedas. In **Buddhism** it marks the Buddha — carved on the soles of his feet, stamped on the chest of statues — and traveled with the faith into China, Korea, and Japan, where it is called the **manji** and where, to this day, it is the standard map symbol for a Buddhist temple. In **Jainism** it is the holiest symbol of all: the emblem of one of the great Tirthankaras, and its four arms represent the four realms where a soul can be reborn — heaven, hell, the human world, and the world of plants and animals — before final liberation. Every Jain temple and holy book must contain it; ceremonies open with rice scattered into a swastika shape.

So there are two facts to hold at once: in Asia, the swastika never stopped being what it always was. It is on maps in Tokyo and taxis in Mumbai to this day. In the West, a single decade — 1920 to 1930 — permanently changed what the symbol meant there.

## Troy, and the Turning Point

Now to the chain of events. In 1871 a self-taught German millionaire-turned-archaeologist named **Heinrich Schliemann** sank an excavation into a mound called Hisarlik on the Turkish coast, chasing Homer's Iliad as if it were a map. He found layer-cake cities, gold, and fame — and, scattered through the pottery of every layer, some **1,800 swastikas**, mostly whirled onto little clay spindle-whorls. Schliemann didn't know what to make of them. So he asked.

Enter **Émile-Louis Burnouf**, director of the French School at Athens — a Sanskrit scholar who also held strongly antisemitic views. In 1872 he wrote to Schliemann that the swastika "should be regarded as a sign of the Aryan race" — adding, in a remark that reveals what was driving the interpretation, that "the Jews have completely rejected it." Schliemann adopted the theory and published it: the Trojans, he declared, were Aryans, and the swastika the badge of a primeval people that had once stretched "from Bactria to Germany," when Germans, Indians, Celts, and Persians were supposedly one nation speaking one language. None of this was so. "Aryan" in Sanskrit meant "noble" or "cultured" — a social and linguistic self-description, not a race. But the idea spread widely. Schliemann put swastikas on the railings, gates, and ceilings of his own grand house in Athens. The word itself entered European print through Schliemann's publications in 1871, gradually replacing the old neutral name *gammadion*.

The wider currents fed it: race-theorists of the mid-nineteenth century, the nationalism of the newly united German Empire of 1871, and the *völkisch* movement's romantic obsession with a supposedly pure Germanic past. By the turn of the century there were secret lodges teaching that the symbol was the ancient property of the Aryans — the **Guido von List** circle (whose "Armanen runes" were published after he claimed they had been revealed to him during eleven months of blindness following cataract surgery in 1902; his High Armanen Order followed in 1911) and the **Germanenorden**, founded in Berlin in 1912, which formally adopted a swastika set upon a cross in 1916. Britannica notes that List himself proposed the swastika as the badge for all antisemitic organizations. A Romanian professor, A. C. Cuza, is documented as the first to deploy it as an explicit emblem of international antisemitism before the First World War. The symbol was being politically repurposed for twenty years before the National Socialist movement adopted it.

## The Good-Luck Decade

Here is the part that startles people. **While the racial-political circles were appropriating it, the rest of the Western world was wearing it for luck.** Right into the 1920s, the swastika was everywhere — as ordinary as a horseshoe:

- **Coca-Cola** issued a brass watch fob in 1925 — a swastika bearing the slogan "Drink Coca-Cola in bottles five cents." Collectors have catalogued **over a thousand different good-luck swastika tokens, coins, and fobs** distributed by ordinary American businesses between 1885 and 1939, most promising luck alongside four-leaf clovers and wishbones.
- **Rudyard Kipling**, the most read English-language author alive, used the swastika as his personal emblem on the covers and flyleaves of his books — his connection to India made it feel natural. His *Just So Stories* include an illustration of a stone bearing a "magic mark" (a swastika) that some later editions quietly altered.
- The **Boy Scouts** put it on their Thanks Badge from 1911; Baden-Powell added it to the Scouting Medal of Merit in 1922 as a token of luck; British Scouters requested a redesign by 1934, and it was changed in 1935.
- During the **First World War** it served as the emblem of Britain's National War Savings Committee, appearing on savings stamps in 1916.
- **American military units** carried swastika insignia in the First World War — and the symbol could still be seen on British RAF aircraft markings as late as 1939.
- A Detroit car maker, **K-R-I-T**, built automobiles from 1909 to 1915 with a white swastika radiator badge. Carlsberg put it on beer bottles. An American pulp-magazine publisher used a blue swastika as his company logo. The Girls' Club of America published a magazine called *Swastika*. Quilt patterns, postcards, golf clubs, a bank's tiled floor in Bolton, England.

**Finland** adopted a blue swastika as its air force roundel in March 1918 — before the National Socialist Party chose the symbol — because a Swedish count, **Eric von Rosen**, had donated Finland's first warplane with his personal good-luck sign painted on the wing (von Rosen, an explorer who later became Göring's brother-in-law, used it as a general talisman). The Finns flew swastika-marked aircraft until 1945, kept it on unit flags and decorations for decades afterward, and have only in recent years — after a century of patient explanation to bewildered allies — begun phasing it out entirely.

All of this existed simultaneously with the political repurposing. The swastika of 1920 meant different things to different audiences at once.

## The Dentist and the Party Emblem

So we arrive at the flag. The **Thule Society** — a *völkisch*-occult lodge founded in Munich in August 1918, named after the mythic northern homeland, meeting in rooms hung with swastika banners — incubated the tiny German Workers' Party that Hitler joined in November 1919. (Hitler himself was never a member of the Thule Society; the popular legend of an all-powerful occult conspiracy steering National Socialism is much exaggerated — the sober scholarly treatment is Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke's *The Occult Roots of Nazism* — but the Thulists did supply men, money, and symbols to the early party.)

In **May 1919**, a member named **Dr. Friedrich Krohn** — a dentist from Starnberg — proposed a party emblem: red cloth, white disk, and in the center a black swastika **with curved legs, facing left**, which Krohn considered the auspicious orientation (the right-facing form, he believed, portended misfortune). The party accepted the concept. Hitler modified the design: he straightened the legs and reversed the direction to right-facing. In *Mein Kampf*, he described arriving at the final form himself — "a flag with a red background, a white disk, and a black swastika in the middle" — after "innumerable attempts," specifying the exact proportions of disk and emblem. In the same chapter he acknowledged the prior submission: "a dentist from Starnberg did deliver a design that was not bad after all... having only the one fault that a swastika with curved legs was composed into a white disk." The record preserves both contributions.

On **August 7, 1920**, at the party congress in Salzburg, the red-white-black swastika banner became the official emblem of the National Socialist Party. Hitler explained the colors as design as well as doctrine — the flag had to work "as a poster" — and laid out the meaning: *"In red we see the social idea of the movement, in white the nationalistic idea, in the swastika the mission of the struggle for the victory of the Aryan man and, by the same token, the victory of the idea of creative work, which as such always has been and always will be anti-Semitic."* Red, white, and black were the colors of the old Imperial flag, reaching back past the Weimar Republic to the Germany millions still felt affection for. The ancient charm of humanity was now the emblem of a political movement on the rise — and it worked exactly as designed.

## From a Banner to the Flag of a State

The rest unfolds on schedule: March 12, 1933 — weeks after our story's beginning — Hindenburg and Hitler decree that the imperial tricolor and the swastika flag fly side by side, "the glorious past of the German Empire" joined to "the powerful rebirth of the German nation." September 15, 1935 — at the Nuremberg rally, on the day the Nuremberg Race Laws pass — the swastika flag becomes the sole national flag of Germany. By January 30, 1939, when Hitler stood in the Kroll Opera House to deliver the speech in this volume, the sign covered the hall in banners the size of walls, and when he told the deputies that the Sudeten Germans had carried "the flag of the National Socialist Reich... in their hearts," the flag on the podium before him was a sign carved by mammoth hunters fifteen thousand years earlier.

## Two Histories Since

After 1945, the West effectively closed the symbol. Germany restricted its public display except in scholarship, education, and religious contexts; other countries adopted their own rules. And yet the story did not end, because the symbol's older lives continued regardless: Jain ceremonies still open with rice swastikas; Hindu weddings still bear them; Japan still marks temples with the manji on every map (in the run-up to the Tokyo Olympics the country quietly debated replacing the map symbol so as not to startle foreign visitors — a debate with no clean answer); Buddhist communities outside India largely switched to the left-facing form after the war to mark the difference. Finland spent a century explaining its blue roundel before choosing the eagle instead.

That is the full answer to where the symbol came from: **it was never one movement's property.** It belonged to Ice Age hunters, to Vedic poets, to Jain monks, to Greek potters, to Navajo weavers, to Finnish pilots, to British savers, to the Boy Scouts, to Coca-Cola's marketing men, and to one dentist in Starnberg with firm opinions about which way a lucky sign should face. For twelve years it was the banner of the Third Reich; for its other fifteen thousand years, it has meant well-being. Both histories are true, and the swastika — one of humanity's oldest marks — continues to carry them both.

# Appendix II: The Origin of the Word 'Nazi'

The party at the center of everything in this volume had a very long official name: the **National Socialist German Workers' Party**. In German that is *Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei*, usually shortened to **NSDAP**. It took that name in **February 1920**, when the small group Hitler had joined a year earlier renamed itself in a Munich beer hall. The name was deliberate: "National" and "Socialist" together, aimed at winning over workers who might otherwise support the Marxist left. And the name was not even new — German workers' parties in Austria had used versions of "National Socialist" before the First World War. Hitler's movement took the label over and made it famous.

Here is the first surprise: **in the movement's early days, its own nickname was not "Nazi" — it was "Naso."** That was the simple shortening of *Na*-tional-*So*-zialist. If you had attended a Munich party meeting in 1919 or 1920, "Naso" is the short word you would have heard. The word the whole world uses today came from the other side — from the people who hated the party.

## How the Nickname Was Born

Bavaria, early 1920s. The National Socialists are rising in Munich, and their enemies — the Social Democrats, the liberal press, the satirists — want a mocking nickname for them. German makes this easy. In the Bavarian and Austrian accent, the sound "-ti-" is pronounced "-tsi-." So the second syllable of *Na-ti-onalsozialist* comes out "Nazi." Clip the word there, and you have a natural, sarcastic short form.

The exact same trick gave the Social Democrats their nickname: *So-tzi-aldemokrat* became **"Sozi"** — a word Germans still use for SPD members today. So "Nazi" and "Sozi" are a matched pair, coined the same way, one for each side of the political fight.

And there was a second joke built in. **"Nazi" was already a common Bavarian and Austrian nickname for the man's name Ignaz (Ignatius)** — and it carried a meaning: the simple country bumpkin, the fool, the hayseed. In old Austrian popular comedy, the dim-witted peasant character was called *Nazi*. So the political nickname cut twice: it shrank the party's grand name down to a joke syllable, *and* it called its members village idiots. Both explanations — the clipped syllable and the bumpkin pun — are on record, and most scholars think they worked together. Who exactly wrote it first is lost; it was born in the mocking press of Munich in the early 1920s.

## The Man Who Carried the Word to the World

The name most often linked to the word is **Konrad Heiden**. His story is worth knowing. He was a Munich journalist and a liberal who covered Hitler's movement from its earliest beer-hall days for the *Frankfurter Zeitung*. He knew the party's beginnings better than almost any outsider; it was even said that Hitler sometimes waited to start meetings until Heiden showed up, because he knew the reporter would be there. Heiden despised the movement and wrote against it, using its enemies' nickname — "Nazi," the Bavarian word for bumpkin — in his articles. His history of National Socialism, published in 1932, was publicly burned by the party just before it took power. When the party took over each country, one of the first things it did there was ban and burn Heiden's books. He fled first to France, then to America.

The New York Times said in his 1966 obituary that Heiden is "sometimes given credit for popularizing the word 'Nazi.'" Whether he coined it or merely spread it, he was certainly its great exporter: through his books, read across Europe and America in the 1930s and 1940s, "Nazi" became the standard name everywhere. An insult from the Munich gutter became the vocabulary of the world's newspapers.

## The Word They Refused

Here is the detail that completes the picture: **the National Socialists never called themselves Nazis.** Not at the start, not at the peak, not even casually. Their own word for themselves was *Nationalsozialist* — National Socialist — or just the abbreviation *NS*, as in NSDAP. In their own speeches, their own newspapers, their own official language, "Nazi" appears only as the enemy's mocking word — and mocking words are not what people call themselves. They never put it on a banner or in their anthem.

This even shows up in the speech at the heart of this volume. In the whole of Hitler's January 30, 1939 address, the word "Nazi" never once appears. He says *nationalsozialistisch*: "the National Socialist state," "the National Socialist people's community." The speech speaks the movement's own formal language. The world's everyday word for that whole world came from the people standing outside the hall.

## 1930, London — The Word Crosses into English

The dictionaries mark the crossing. The **Oxford English Dictionary's earliest example of "Nazi" in English is from 1930, in the Times of London.** Merriam-Webster also dates the first English use to 1930. "Nazism" appeared the same year. So the nickname entered the world's most-used language just as the movement was about to take over a country — perfect timing for a word built to become a headline. Through the 1930s it spread through exile newspapers, refugee memoirs, and foreign correspondents' reports. By 1945 something unusual had happened: the winners' slang had completely replaced the movement's own name. Nobody says "the Naso era." Everybody says the Nazis.

## After 1945 — Two Different Lives

After the war, the word settled into different roles in different languages. English took "Nazi" as the plain, everyday historical term — as neutral now as "Roman." German, the language the insult was coined in, kept more of its rough edge: formal German prefers the dignified words — *der Nationalsozialismus* (National Socialism), *die NS-Zeit* (the Nazi era), *der Nationalsozialist* (the National Socialist) — while "Nazi" survives mostly in casual speech, a little harsher, closer to its gutter origins. English speakers say "Nazi" the way they would say "Roman." Germans say it the way you might say "thug."

Then English did one more remarkable thing with the word. Over time, far from its original meaning, **"nazi" turned into a general insult for any bossy rule-enforcer**: a "grammar Nazi," a "health Nazi," a "soup Nazi" — that last one from a famous 1995 *Seinfeld* episode that fixed the new meaning in American speech. Dictionaries now list the sense formally: "a person who is fanatically dedicated to or seeks to regulate a specified activity." Some people find this drift insulting to history's victims, and that objection deserves respect. Others point out it only confirms what the word always was: not a proud name, but a weapon of mockery — now aimed at anyone.

## Where the Word Stands

So, in plain summary: **"Nazi" is a clipped syllable of "Nationalsozialist," shaped by a Bavarian accent; before the party existed it was a bumpkin's nickname for every Ignaz in Austria; in the early 1920s the party's enemies in Munich turned it into a sneer; a journalist whose books were burned in every country the movement conquered carried it to the world; it entered English in a London newspaper in 1930; it replaced the movement's own name entirely in victory; and it ended its first century as one of the most flexible insults in the English language.** It rhymes with the story of the swastika — the movement's two most famous legacies, its symbol and its very name, both came from somewhere else. One of them was handed to it by its enemies.

And the honest footnote, as always: no one is recorded as the first person to write "Nazi" as a sneer; the claims that Heiden coined it come hedged ("it was said," "sometimes given credit"), and while the Ignatz bumpkin usage is well documented, it cannot be proven whether it was the *main* source or simply a pun layered on top of the clipped syllable. What is solid: the word came from the movement's enemies, the movement refused it, English adopted it in 1930, and the rest is the journey you have just read.
